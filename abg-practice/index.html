Toolkit

Predict, then compare with what the sheet measured. Inside the range = appropriate compensation; outside = a second disorder.

1 · Is the sheet valid?

[H⁺] = 24 × PaCO₂ ÷ HCO₃⁻

  • [H⁺] ≈ 80 − pH digits (pH 7.20–7.50)
  • Otherwise [H⁺] = 10^(9 − pH)
  • pH = 6.1 + log [HCO₃⁻ ÷ (0.03 × PaCO₂)]

2–3 · Normal values and primary process

  • pH 7.35–7.45 · PaCO₂ 35–45 (40) · HCO₃⁻ 22–26 (24)
  • pH ↓ + HCO₃⁻ ↓ → metabolic acidosis
  • pH ↓ + PaCO₂ ↑ → respiratory acidosis
  • pH ↑ + HCO₃⁻ ↑ → metabolic alkalosis
  • pH ↑ + PaCO₂ ↓ → respiratory alkalosis

5 · Anion gap

AG = Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻)

  • Normal 12 ± 4
  • Add 2.5 for every 10 g/L albumin below 40
  • > 16 → high-gap acidosis (GOLD MARK)

5 · Delta ratio

(AG − 12) ÷ (24 − HCO₃⁻)

  • < 0.8 → HAGMA + NAGMA
  • 0.8–2 → pure HAGMA
  • > 2 → HAGMA + metabolic alkalosis

6 · Osmolar gap

Measured − (2 × Na⁺ + Glu ÷ 18 + BUN ÷ 2.8)

  • Glucose and BUN in mg/dL
  • > 10 abnormal · > 20 → toxic alcohol

6 · Oxygenation

PAO₂ = FiO₂ × 713 − PaCO₂ ÷ 0.8

  • Room air ≈ 150 − 1.25 × PaCO₂
  • A–a = PAO₂ − PaO₂ (normal ≈ age ÷ 4 + 4)
  • P/F = PaO₂ ÷ FiO₂: ≤ 300 mild · ≤ 200 moderate · ≤ 100 severe

6 · Urine tests

UAG = U-Na⁺ + U-K⁺ − U-Cl⁻

  • Negative → gut loss · Positive → RTA
  • Urine Cl⁻ < 20 → saline-responsive alkalosis
  • Urine Cl⁻ > 20 → saline-resistant

6 · Content and conversions

CaO₂ = 1.34 × Hb × SaO₂ + 0.003 × PaO₂

  • Glucose mg/dL ÷ 18 = mmol/L
  • Creatinine mg/dL × 88.4 = µmol/L
  • Nasal O₂: FiO₂ ≈ 0.21 + 0.04 × L/min
